Phoenix Renaissance Faire.

Phoenix Renaissance Faire is a digital design project focused on creating a clearer, more immersive way for visitors to explore event information, activities, and the overall faire experience.

Role

Web Designer
UI/UX Designer

Website / Event Experience

Focus

Overview

An experience that starts before the gates open.

The website needed to help visitors plan their day while also building excitement around the faire itself.

Event websites often have to balance a large amount of practical information with strong visual storytelling.

Phoenix Renaissance Faire was designed to make that information easier to browse while maintaining a themed and engaging visual experience.

The challenge

Lots to explore, without getting lost.

Visitors may be looking for schedules, activities, tickets, attractions, food, entertainment, or general event information.

Without a clear structure, that amount of content can quickly become overwhelming.

The design challenge was to organize the experience so visitors could quickly find what they needed while still enjoying the personality and atmosphere of the faire.

Design goals

Useful first, immersive second.

01

Clear Structure

Organize content in a way that makes navigation easy to understand at a glance.

02

Event Discovery

Make attractions and activities easy to browse so visitors can plan around what interests them most.

03

Themed Visuals

Build excitement through imagery and visual storytelling without sacrificing readability.

04

Mobile Access

Ensure important event information remains accessible on phones while visitors are on the go.

User needs

Built around the visitor journey.

The design was approached from the perspective of visitors planning before the event and checking information during it.

Before attending, visitors may want to understand what the faire offers, when it takes place, and how to prepare.

During the event, they may need faster access to schedules, attractions, or other practical details.

The interface needed to support both moments without requiring visitors to relearn the site.

Information architecture

Making the content feel lighter.

A strong content hierarchy helps a complex event site feel much easier to use.

Related information was grouped into clearer categories so visitors would not have to search through long or unrelated sections.

This structure also supports future growth as new activities, attractions, or event details are added over time.

Visual direction

A little fantasy, without the clutter.

The visual design uses expressive imagery and themed elements to reinforce the Renaissance-faire atmosphere.

Decorative choices are balanced by cleaner content areas, ensuring text and navigation remain easy to read.

The result is a site that feels visually connected to the event while still working like a modern digital experience.

Page design

Consistency across every stop.

Shared layout patterns help visitors understand how information is presented across the site.

Repeated heading styles, content blocks, navigation patterns, and calls to action create a more predictable browsing experience.

This consistency allows themed visuals to remain expressive without making each page feel disconnected from the rest.

Responsive design

Designed to travel with the visitor.

Mobile usability is especially important for an event site because visitors may need information while they are already at the faire.

Layouts, content, navigation, and imagery adapt for smaller screens so visitors can continue using the experience without excessive scrolling or searching.

The responsive design keeps practical information accessible while preserving the visual personality of the desktop site.

Final result

Easier planning, stronger anticipation.

The final Phoenix Renaissance Faire concept brings practical event planning and immersive visual storytelling together in one experience.

Visitors can navigate information more clearly while still getting a sense of the event's personality before attending.

The design creates a flexible foundation that can support event updates, new attractions, and continued growth.

Reflection

Experience begins before arrival.

Phoenix Renaissance Faire reinforced the importance of designing around the full experience rather than only individual screens.

Event websites need to communicate information clearly, but they also have an opportunity to establish tone, anticipation, and emotional connection.

The project strengthened my approach to responsive design, content hierarchy, visual storytelling, and designing around different moments in the user journey.
