Suklaa Chocolatiera.

Suklaa Chocolatiera is a Finnish-inspired chocolate brand created for the U.S. market, combining Nordic craftsmanship, family tradition, sustainability, and imaginative visual storytelling.

Overview

Finnish heritage, reimagined.

Suklaa was created to introduce Finnish chocolate craft to a U.S. audience without losing the warmth and history behind the brand.

The identity draws inspiration from Nordic craftsmanship, nature, family tradition, and the sense of discovery that comes with handcrafted chocolate.

The goal was to create a brand that felt premium and imaginative while still remaining approachable, comforting, and rooted in tradition.

The challenge

Tradition without feeling dated.

Suklaa needed to communicate heritage and craftsmanship without becoming overly traditional or visually predictable.

The challenge was to balance Finnish influences with a contemporary visual system that could appeal to customers unfamiliar with the culture behind the brand.

The identity also needed enough flexibility to support multiple chocolate flavors, packaging variations, digital experiences, and future brand growth.

Brand strategy

Craft meets transformation.

Suklaa combines the Creator and Magician brand archetypes.

The Creator supports craftsmanship, originality, and the handmade quality of the chocolate.

The Magician introduces transformation, discovery, and a sense of wonder — helping the product feel like an experience rather than simply another chocolate bar.

Together, the archetypes support Suklaa's four core values: transformation, craftsmanship, sustainability, and imagination.

Visual identity

Rich, Nordic, and magical.

Suklaa's identity combines deep, atmospheric colors with warmer tones that reference chocolate, craftsmanship, and light.

Velvet Cocoa and Starfall Navy establish depth and sophistication, while Twilight Indigo adds a more imaginative and unexpected element.

Honeyed Gold introduces warmth and a premium feeling, while Whipped Cream and Mint Green soften the system and provide balance.

Typography

Expressive meets approachable.

Typography helps balance Suklaa's imaginative personality with the clarity needed for a functional brand system.

Lust is used for headlines and expressive brand moments, giving the identity a premium and crafted character.

Raleway supports the system as the primary body typeface, providing readability and a clean modern contrast.

Product line

One brand, many flavors.

Suklaa's product system was designed to give each chocolate bar its own personality while maintaining a recognizable family resemblance.

The product line includes Aurora, Toffee, Blueberry, Mint, Caramel, and Cookies & Cream.

Each flavor can introduce its own visual cues and accents while still using the larger Suklaa identity system to maintain consistency.

Web design

Extending the brand into a digital space.

The website carries Suklaa's premium visual identity into an approachable digital experience.

The site was structured around Home, About, Contact, Product Showcase, and Community pages.

The design prioritizes strong imagery, brand storytelling, product discovery, and enough flexibility to support Suklaa as the product line grows.

Final identity

Tradition with a little wonder.

The final Suklaa identity creates a flexible visual world rooted in heritage without being limited by it.

The brand balances Nordic craft, warmth, sophistication, sustainability, and imagination across packaging and digital touchpoints.

The resulting system is designed to feel cohesive enough to build recognition while remaining flexible enough for future products and experiences.

Reflection

Building more than a logo.

Suklaa strengthened my understanding of how brand strategy influences every visual decision that follows.

Rather than treating the logo, packaging, color palette, and website as separate deliverables, the project required each piece to support the same overall brand personality.

The process helped me think more intentionally about scalability, consistency, storytelling, and how a visual identity can adapt across both physical and digital experiences.
