Featured character

Story first. Design second.

Because these characters belong to a larger story, I design them around who they are before focusing on how they look. Clothing, expression, silhouette, color, and fantasy details all become opportunities to communicate personality and history.

Mavis — whose true name is Verda — is one of the central characters in The Other Side. Her design balances softness, mystery, and the supernatural elements tied to her identity.

My approach

Turning story into visual language.

01

Personality

Each design begins with the character's temperament, motivations, relationships, and role in the story.

02

World

Clothing, materials, weapons, wings, magic, and other details connect the character to the world they inhabit.

03

Symbolism

Color, shape, and recurring visual motifs reinforce themes, powers, relationships, and hidden identity.

04

Story Growth

Designs can evolve alongside the character so visual changes reflect what they experience in the narrative.

Original IP

Designing a cast that belongs together.

The characters in The Other Side are designed as part of the same world, but each one still needs a distinct identity.

I use repeated visual language — materials, fantasy motifs, color relationships, and costume structure — to connect the cast while allowing individual silhouettes and details to communicate each character's role.

As the novel continues to develop, these designs can evolve alongside the story and become a visual record of how the characters change.
