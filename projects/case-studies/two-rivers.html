Two Rivers Renaissance Faire.

Two Rivers Renaissance Faire is a website design project focused on making event information easier to discover while creating a visual experience that reflects the atmosphere and personality of the faire.

Overview

Bringing the faire to the screen.

The website needed to function as both an information source and an introduction to the Two Rivers Renaissance Faire experience.

Visitors need quick access to practical information such as dates, attractions, activities, and event details, but the website also needs enough personality to communicate the atmosphere of the faire itself.

The project focused on creating a clearer, more engaging experience that balances usability with a strong visual theme.

The challenge

Information without the overwhelm.

Event websites often contain a large amount of information, making clear hierarchy and navigation especially important.

Visitors may be looking for different things — schedules, attractions, general information, or details that help them decide whether they want to attend.

The challenge was to organize that information in a way that felt easy to explore without losing the immersive personality expected from a Renaissance faire.

Information architecture

Helping visitors find their way.

The structure of the website needed to support visitors who may arrive with very different goals.

Navigation and page hierarchy were organized around the information people are most likely to look for when planning an event visit.

Breaking information into clearer sections helps reduce the amount of scanning required and allows visitors to move through the site more naturally.

User experience

Designing around visitor questions.

The interface was approached from the perspective of someone preparing to visit the faire.

Instead of expecting visitors to search through large blocks of information, the experience emphasizes recognizable categories, readable content, and clear visual hierarchy.

Important calls to action and event information are given stronger visual weight so visitors can quickly understand what to do next.

Visual direction

Modern structure, faire personality.

The visual design needed to feel themed without sacrificing clarity or turning every element into decoration.

Large imagery and expressive design elements help establish the Renaissance-faire atmosphere, while cleaner layout structures keep the actual content readable.

This balance allows the experience to feel immersive while still functioning like a practical event website.

Page design

A consistent experience.

Reusable design patterns help the site remain consistent as visitors move between different types of content.

Headings, content sections, imagery, navigation, and call-to-action styles work together to establish a recognizable visual rhythm.

Keeping these patterns consistent also makes the site easier to expand as additional event information is added.

Responsive design

Ready for the road.

Event information needs to remain easy to access when visitors are away from a desktop computer.

The responsive experience adapts layouts, navigation, imagery, and spacing for smaller screens while keeping the most important information accessible.

This allows visitors to browse the site while planning at home or checking event details from a phone.

Final result

Easier to explore, harder to forget.

The final website concept combines practical event information with a stronger visual experience.

Clearer hierarchy and navigation make it easier for visitors to discover what the faire has to offer, while the visual direction gives the event a more memorable digital presence.

The result is an experience designed to support both event planning and excitement before the visitor ever reaches the faire itself.

Reflection

Designing for the whole visit.

Two Rivers reinforced how closely information architecture and visual design depend on one another.

A themed website still needs a strong structural foundation, especially when visitors rely on it to make practical decisions.

The project helped strengthen my approach to navigation, hierarchy, responsive layouts, and creating experiences that feel expressive without sacrificing usability.
